Vendor B Managed Database — Incident Postmortem

Incident ID: VB-2026-0418-3
Date: 2026-04-18
Start time: 09:03 UTC
Region: eu-west-1
Duration: primary failover took 6 minutes 40 seconds against a 30 second
target
Severity: SEV-2

Summary
-------
At 09:03 UTC on 2026-04-18, the primary node of a managed database cluster
in eu-west-1 became unresponsive due to underlying host hardware
degradation. Automatic failover to the standby replica was triggered, but
completed in 6 minutes 40 seconds rather than the expected 30 seconds.
During the extended failover window, approximately 4% of write requests to
the cluster timed out.

Root Cause
----------
The failover mechanism relies on a DNS record update to redirect client
connections from the old primary endpoint to the newly promoted replica.
Investigation found that a caching layer in front of the DNS resolvers was
serving a stale record with a longer-than-configured effective TTL,
causing a large share of clients to keep resolving to the unresponsive
old primary well after the promotion completed.

Impact
------
Approximately 4% of write requests issued to the cluster during the 6
minute 40 second window timed out and returned an error to the calling
application. Read replicas were not affected. No data loss occurred; the
newly promoted primary retained all committed writes.

Remediation
-----------
Engineers manually flushed the affected DNS caching layer at 09:08 UTC,
which restored correct resolution and ended the elevated timeout rate.

Commitments
-----------
Vendor B is correcting the effective TTL enforcement in the caching layer
so that configured DNS TTL values are honored, and is adding a
non-DNS-dependent connection-redirect mechanism as a second failover path
for future incidents of this kind.
