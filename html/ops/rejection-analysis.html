Rejection Analysis — every real-claim rejection in the 55-worker swarm run

Source: scenarios/incident_monitoring_swarm/verification_log.json (234 entries), the second 55-worker run. Every one of the 29 real-claim rejections below was read individually and checked against the source postmortem file — none of this is inferred from the aggregate count.
✓ Fixed and re-validated — see Move 1 re-validation: the unicode-normalization gap and the arithmetic-recomputation check both landed and were confirmed against two live 55-worker re-runs (a real crash found and fixed along the way). The quote-paraphrasing problem below, however, is still present in the re-run — the anti-echo prompt fix did not collapse it, and a related new failure mode (the model misusing the new computation field) appeared alongside it. This page's numbers below are the original pre-fix run; see the Move 1 page for the current, post-fix breakdown.
"~12.7% rejection rate" (29 of 229 real claims) is the number the raw log gives you. It is not the number that answers "how often is the swarm wrong." The deterministic verifier rejects a claim the instant its quote field fails an exact byte-for-byte substring match against the source — and 27 of these 29 rejections are the model's quote field being malformed (ellipsis, splicing, or literally echoing the prompt's placeholder text) while the underlying text claim is true. Only 2 of 29 are genuine content problems.

Reading it the other way: the swarm's real, content-level error rate on this run was 2 claims out of 229 ≈ 0.9%, not 12.7%. The other 11.8 percentage points are the fail-closed verifier doing exactly what it's designed to do — refusing to confirm anything it can't byte-match — applied against a worker that doesn't reliably produce a clean verbatim quote. That's a real defect (it throws away true claims and burns judge-fallback calls), but it's a worker prompt/parsing defect, not a verifier or factual-accuracy defect.

Worked examples — quote-formatting artifacts (claim was true)

Ellipsis truncation — solace_queue_r4, 8 of this run's 12 ellipsis cases came from a single worker call:
text: "Consumer lag grew to 340,000 unacknowledged messages at peak." quote: "Consumer lag grew to approximately 340,000 unacknowledged messages at peak..." source (verbatim): "Consumer lag grew to approximately 340,000 unacknowledged messages at peak, delaying order-event delivery..."
The quoted span is real and correctly located — the model just appended ... where the sentence continues, and the deterministic check requires the quote string to appear verbatim, so the trailing ellipsis makes it a non-match.

Placeholder echo — every ridgeline_auth rejection except one (6 of 11 repeats) shows this exact pattern:
text: "The misconfigured batch job caused database connection pool exhaustion." quote: "a verbatim quote from the source supporting this claim, or null if none applies"
That string is not from the source — it's copied verbatim from the worker's own extraction-prompt schema description. The model filled the quote field with the instructions for the field instead of following them. ridgeline_auth alone accounts for 6 of the 10 placeholder-echo cases; cobalt_storage_r6 has the 7th.

Sentence splice — meridian_cache_r6:
text: "The backlog was caused by a message-broker partition rebalance that paused invalidation delivery for 11 minutes." quote: "Starting at 03:14 UTC on 2026-05-11, Meridian Cache's edge nodes in the ap-south-1 region began serving stale content due to a cache-invalidation message backlog. The backlog was caused by a message-broker partition rebalance that paused invalidation delivery for 11 minutes."
The second sentence is a real, correct quote on its own; the model prepended an unrelated earlier sentence to it, so the combined string never appears contiguously in the source.

Typographic mismatch — solace_queue_r11, confirmed by reading the raw source bytes:
text: "The queue's retention window was never approached." quote (model, U+2019): "the queue’s retention window of 72 hours was never approached." source (actual, U+0027): "the queue's retention window of 72 hours was never approached."
Content is 100% correct; the model "smart-quoted" the apostrophe and the exact-match check has no tolerance for it.

Worked examples — genuine content errors (2 of 29)

Numeric/arithmetic error — solace_queue_r11:
text: "The backlog drain completed within 17 minutes after throttling the partner integration." source: "...throttled the specific partner's webhook concurrency at 12:11 UTC, which stabilized lag within 8 minutes. Full backlog drain completed at 12:52 UTC."
12:52 − 12:11 = 41 minutes, not 17. The model appears to have reported the 8-minute lag-stabilization figure mislabeled as the drain time, or simply miscalculated. This is a real extraction error the verifier was right to reject — it just happened to also fail on a quote-format technicality (no quote round-trip), so method: deterministic caught it before llm_judge ever needed to reason about the number.

Hallucinated detail, caught by llm_judge — ridgeline_auth_r5 (this one had no quote, so it went through the LLM-judge path, which worked correctly):
text: "The batch job was killing alerts at 20:14 UTC and took about 10 minutes to recover." judge reason: "The claim is not supported by the source text because it incorrectly states that the batch job was 'killing alerts' at 20:14 UTC."
Source says the batch job itself was identified and killed (terminated) via connection-pool alerts at 20:14 UTC. The model inverted subject and object of "kill." This is the one case in the whole 234-claim run where the LLM-judge fallback caught something the deterministic path structurally could not (no quote existed to check).

What this changes about "accuracy kitni hai"

Two different numbers answer two different questions:

"How often does the verifier confirm a claim?" — 200/229 = 87.3%. This is what you'd see in a dashboard and it's the number that determines judge-fallback call volume and Memory write-through rate.

"How often is the underlying extracted fact actually wrong?" — 2/229 ≈ 0.9%. This is what "accuracy" means to a human reading the postmortems, and it's the number that should drive any claim about the swarm being trustworthy or not.

The gap between those two numbers (≈11.8 points) is entirely attributable to one fixable thing: the extraction worker's prompt does not reliably teach the model to (a) never echo the schema description, (b) never truncate a quote with an ellipsis, and (c) never splice two spans together. Fixing that prompt should collapse the raw rejection rate toward the true ~1% content-error rate without weakening the verifier at all — the fail-closed design itself is working exactly as intended (0 fabrications slipped through, 5/5 caught).

Also worth noting

All 5 injected fabrications were rejected, split 3 deterministic / 2 llm_judge as designed (fabrications with a fake quote hit the deterministic path; fabrications with no quote fell through to the judge). None of the 5 fabrications appear in the 29-rejection breakdown above — this page covers only the 29 rejections among the 229 real, non-injected claims.
